Studio and Mobile Industry Tycoon Framework

A shared game design for the current studio simulator and a future cell phone company simulator

November 1 2026

Build a believable product company simulator around the same satisfying cycle in both settings: choose an opportunity, commit a budget, make development tradeoffs, release a product, watch its sales, learn from the results, and reinvest. The immediate goal is a stronger game studio simulation. The next application is a cell phone company spanning the analog, feature phone, smartphone, and app ecosystem eras.

What this document decides

• Keep one shared company, project, research, staff, event, calendar, save, and sales foundation. Put industry-specific content and rules into separate data and simulation adapters.

• Use recognizable near-name companies and products with original illustrations and writing. Store stable internal IDs so display-name changes do not break saved studios.

• Prioritize visible cause and effect. The player should understand what changed in quality, demand, cost, and reputation after a decision.

• Treat the phone version as a hardware business with inventory, manufacturing, suppliers, carriers, returns, and software support. Those systems are new work, not features already present in Afterlight.

Current deliverable and future scope

Afterlight 2.1 adds weekly sales bars, release selection, copies and net-receipts views, a weekly ledger, release profit tracking, and closer hardware parody names. The existing office, staged development, engines, staff, reviews, and hardware timeline remain playable. This document outlines the next framework and the phone adaptation; it does not claim a playable phone-company mode exists.

The core player promise

I can recognize the industry I am competing in, make meaningful product decisions, see the business consequences, and build a company whose strengths reflect my choices. The simulation should support different viable strategies rather than require one memorized recipe.

What appears to be missing

The remaining gap is connected consequences and pacing. A successful release should visibly change the studio, unlock a tempting next step, and create a new financial decision. A weak release should explain its weaknesses and offer a recoverable next move. A graph alone improves readability; the surrounding sales commentary, meaningful rivals, milestone timing, and visible production feedback make it feel like a complete business game.

Use independently designed numbers and original content throughout. Describe similarities at the level of functional mechanics. Record uncertain observations as research questions rather than claiming exact parity with Game Dev Tycoon.

Industry adapter contract

Each industry defines its project types, stage disciplines, output metrics, technology tree, historical catalog, customer segments, channels, cost model, review criteria, and event conditions. Shared screens consume consistent project, release, transaction, and timeline records. Industry rules decide what a good product means and how it reaches customers.

Minimum data records

Keep CompanyState, Person, Project, Technology, MarketEntity, Release, WeeklySales, EventDefinition, and KnowledgeRecord as separate concepts. Use stable IDs, explicit units, a save version, and source notes for historical entries. A weekly transaction should distinguish customer demand, actual units sold, gross revenue, channel fees, cost of goods, returns, and cash received.

Simulation order

Advance the calendar, resolve market and technology changes, complete work, evaluate product availability, calculate demand, fulfill sales, settle transactions and overhead, update reputation and knowledge, then issue notifications. Paused decisions must not consume time. Saving and restoring the same random state must reproduce the next outcome.

Sales display and accounting contract

Use a persistent compact sales chart in the studio and a full report for every release. One vertical bar represents one sales week. The current game records 20 weeks; the framework should make the number of reporting periods configurable for other industries.

• Show copies sold by default, then let the player switch to net receipts. Label the axes and distinguish recorded weeks, the latest week, and unrecorded future periods. Future placeholders are not a forecast.

• Expose exact values on hover and keyboard focus. Include a weekly table, lifetime copies, receipts, release profit, last-week sales, peak week, and change from the prior week.

• Do not fabricate weekly history for older imports that only contain totals. Show their totals and state that no weekly breakdown was saved.

Accounting definitions

In the current game, net receipts equal copies sold times price, less the storefront share. Release profit subtracts its development budget and the studio operating costs incurred during that development. It is a release contribution measure; company cash also includes research, engine work, recruitment and other spending.

For phones, separate customer demand from fulfilled sales. Inventory purchases reduce cash and create inventory; cost of goods is recognized as units sell. Do not subtract manufacturing cash purchases a second time when calculating profit. Track returns, channel margins, warranty reserves, and support costs separately.

These are proposed catalog entries, not verified historical product records. The source catalog should retain the real-world reference separately for research and review.

The early Simon chronology needs careful labeling: museum catalog and exhibit sources use different early-1990s dates. Use a sourced commercial-availability entry and preserve prototype or announcement dates separately [7].

Cell phone company gameplay outline

Begin as a small handset developer and grow into a manufacturer and software ecosystem company. The player can emphasize affordable volume, premium design, business communication, photography, regional distribution, or durable long-life devices. Rival firms and network changes should create pressure without forcing the player to repeat the historical winners' decisions.

Development stageThree priority areasOutput and tradeoffs
Hardware architectureRadio and chipset; battery and power; industrial designConnectivity, performance, endurance, weight and component cost
Software and experienceOperating system; interface and input; apps and servicesUsability, ecosystem reach, developer support and maintenance burden
Product readinessCamera and media; manufacturing quality; reliability and approvalsReview appeal, yield, defects, returns and launch readiness

Systems that must be added

• Suppliers and manufacturing: component availability, bill of materials, tooling costs, factory capacity, yield, lead times and committed orders.

• Inventory and channels: finished stock, sell-through, carrier commitments, retailer margins, direct sales, regional demand and stockouts.

• Platforms and ecosystems: licensed or proprietary operating systems, application compatibility, developer incentives and a supported installed base.

• Lifecycle support: defects, warranty returns, software updates, security-support commitments, repairability and replacement cycles.

Product decision sequence

Choose a segment, price band, form factor, network generation, operating-system strategy and launch region. Make stage tradeoffs, order a pilot batch, test reliability, commit a manufacturing run, negotiate channels, launch, and monitor sales, inventory and support. Feedback should explain both market response and operational consequences.

Era gates

Use distinct analog, digital feature-phone, mobile web, early smartphone, touch ecosystem, LTE, and 5G eras. Technology can be researched ahead of adoption, but unsupported networks, costly components and weak software ecosystems should limit premature products. The full historical campaign can cover 1983 to 2025 with configurable time compression.

Delivery sequence and acceptance criteria

First complete the studio feedback loop

This revision delivers the weekly sales bars, release reports and closer hardware labels. Next, improve causal release commentary, visible staff workload, milestone timing, and research dependency previews. Replace the current chart-position estimate with rankings derived from simulated rival releases. Add competitor profiles and market events before expanding into more late-game modes.

Then extract the shared foundation

Move industry catalogs and rules behind adapters while retaining the current save and regression tests. Keep office rendering, project planning, sales reporting and business-state transitions independently testable. The first adapter extraction should preserve the studio game's behavior rather than change its balance and structure simultaneously.

Build one phone vertical slice

Prototype the 2002 to 2009 transition with feature phones, business messaging devices, camera phones, touch smartphones, and app ecosystems. Include one supplier pool, three customer segments, two channel types, three competing firms, manufacturing inventory, returns, and a complete sales report. Once this loop works, extend the catalog backward to 1983 and forward through 5G.

Acceptance checks

• Every sales bar equals its recorded weekly transaction. Units, receipts, cumulative totals and profit reconcile with the ledger.

• Sales views work before the first sale, after retirement, with negative profit, on a phone screen, and with keyboard navigation.

• Two materially different development plans produce explainably different results. Reports name the player's strongest and weakest decisions.

• Phone sales never exceed available inventory. Stockouts, returns, yields and component spending update the correct cash and profit accounts.

• At least three phone-company strategies survive the complete slice. Historical disruption is a challenge, not an unavoidable scripted defeat.

• Historical records cite primary or museum sources and distinguish dates, regions, and modeled values. Save migrations preserve company and release history.
